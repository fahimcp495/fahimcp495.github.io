Research

I am interested in what happens to well-understood algorithms when they meet real systems. Classical algorithms are usually analyzed offline, with the whole input available and an exact answer required. In practice, data may arrive over time, computation may need to run in parallel, and an exact answer may cost more than it is worth.

Interests

  • Algorithm engineering. Designing, implementing and evaluating algorithms with both analysis and measurement.
  • Combinatorial optimization and graph algorithms. Finding the structure in a problem and using it to reduce work.
  • Online and parallel algorithms. Adapting classical algorithms to settings where data arrives continuously or computation is spread across processors.
  • Approximation and quality-versus-cost trade-offs. How much solution quality is worth giving up for speed or memory.
  • High-performance computing. How memory access, parallel execution and hardware utilization shape which algorithm works best in practice.

Questions I want to study

  1. When can an exact algorithm be replaced by an approximate, parallel or online one, and what is lost?
  2. Can that loss be bounded with provable guarantees, and where are guarantees out of reach?
  3. How do memory access, parallel execution and hardware utilization change which algorithm is best in practice?

I am still building depth in the theory of approximation and online algorithms. These questions are where I want to do that.
